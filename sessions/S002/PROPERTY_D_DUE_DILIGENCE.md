# S002 Property D due-diligence and novelty/status audit

Date of bounded search: 2026-10-02. Selected target: CAND-01, rank-two Property D for C_n^2.

This record distinguishes source results from bounded search observations. Search failure is never treated as an openness or novelty proof. No source proof was independently audited and no programme proof work occurred.

## Exact definition and target

For a finite abelian group G, s(G) is the least length forcing a zero-sum subsequence of length exp(G). For G=C_n^2, s(C_n^2)=4n-3. Property D says that every sequence S over C_n^2 of length s(G)-1=4n-4 having no zero-sum subsequence of length n is of the form T^(n-1). Since |S|=4(n-1), T has length four, so in the original rank-two formulation S consists of four support elements, each repeated n-1 times.

The original rank-two statement is W. D. Gao, Two Zero-Sum Problems and Multiple Properties, J. Number Theory 81 (2000), 254-265, DOI 10.1006/jnth.1999.2459, Conjecture 0.2. The modern Property-D language and general C_n^r formulation are in W. Gao, A. Geroldinger and W. A. Schmid, Inverse zero-sum problems, Acta Arith. 128.3 (2007).

## Multiplicativity and prime reduction

Gao's 2000 result is multiplicative: if C_m^2 and C_n^2 have Property D, then C_mn^2 has Property D. No coprimality hypothesis is needed. Schmid's 2012 synthesis explicitly records this and says it reduces rank-two Property D to prime moduli.

Therefore an all-prime theorem implies the all-integer theorem, and any composite whose prime factors are all known Property-D primes is already settled.

## Known cases before 2025

B. Sury and R. Thangadurai, Gao's conjecture on zero-sum sequences, Proc. Indian Acad. Sci. Math. Sci. 112 (2002), 399-414, prove the prime p=7 case in Theorem 3.1. Wolfgang A. Schmid, Restricted inverse zero-sum problems in groups of rank two, Q. J. Math. 63 (2012), 477-487, DOI 10.1093/qmath/haq042, Section 3, records that Property D is known for m<=10 and, by multiplicativity, for every m with no prime divisor greater than 7.

These are baseline results, not novel targets.

## 2025 sufficiently-large-prime result

Jan-Christoph Schlage-Puchta, All large primes have Property D, arXiv:2509.02436v1, submitted 2025-09-02, proves that there exists a constant p_0 such that every prime p>p_0 has Property D. The theorem statement is existential and gives no numerical value of p_0. As checked on 2026-10-02, arXiv still lists only v1.

This changes the geometry of the selected target: together with multiplicativity, any unsettled residue of the full all-n conjecture is confined to finitely many exceptional primes below an unspecified cutoff. The paper itself does not identify that finite list.

## Older large-prime announcement

A 2010 Colloquium on Combinatorics abstract by Schlage-Puchta, Additive Combinatorics and Convex Geometry, explicitly described as joint work with G. Bhowmik, already announced as an application that every large prime has Property D. Source: Saarbruecken abstract booklet, 12-13 November 2010, https://www.kolkom.de/download/abstracts/2010-Saarbruecken-Abstracts.pdf.

Accordingly the large-prime conclusion itself is not novel to 2025 as a public announcement. The 2025 preprint is treated here as the current full written result. The bounded search did not locate an earlier full paper proving the 2010 announcement; that non-hit is an access/status observation only.

Searches covered the exact 2025 title and arXiv identifier, Property D plus rank-two notation, 4n-4, 4p-4, Gao's conjecture, inverse EGZ terminology, forward citations, and recent zero-sum preprints. Those are bounded search observations. They do not certify that the remaining cases are open or that a proposed partial result is novel. ## Exact surviving target scope

The programme retains the owner's full CAND-01 identity: establish Property D for all C_n^2. Operationally, after the 2025 theorem the surviving research frontier is to close or structurally eliminate the finite exceptional-prime layer below Schlage-Puchta's existential threshold, ideally through a theorem that completes all primes/all n rather than a merely mechanical isolated check.

This is not an openness claim. Before proof work, the external proposal reviewer must be asked whether there is a known completion, unpublished overlap, an explicit cutoff, later improvement, or standard formulation that narrows or retires this target. Any such evidence overrides the bounded search.

## Retirement/narrowing triggers

Retire or materially narrow CAND-01 if a primary source or credible expert supplies: an all-prime completion; an effective cutoff plus settled verification of every remaining prime; or earlier work subsuming the intended structural advance. Treat a single new exceptional-prime case as insufficient for the intended E-JC route unless it carries a conceptually reusable argument or closes the complete residual set.
